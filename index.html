01

The motion

Secondary schools should adopt a classical education model rather than a progressive one.

Is education about passing on humanity’s best knowledge—or giving students the freedom to discover what knowledge means?

02

About the debate

More than choosing a side.

A debate is a structured exchange of ideas. Each team presents a clear case, supports it with reasoning and examples, challenges the opposing case, and responds to criticism. The goal is not simply to speak louder—it is to think more clearly.

01

Define the motion

Decide what “classical,” “progressive,” and “adopt” mean. Fair definitions keep both teams focused on the same question.

02

Build a case

Make two or three strong claims. For each one, explain why it is true, give an example, and show why it matters.

03

Create real clash

Answer the other team’s strongest point directly. Compare impacts: which model is fairer, more effective, and better for students?

The core question

Should school first transmit a shared body of knowledge—or help every student construct knowledge through experience?

Before you speak:

  • Know your team’s central claim.
  • Prepare evidence and concrete examples.
  • Predict the best opposing argument.
  • Finish with the impact on real students.
